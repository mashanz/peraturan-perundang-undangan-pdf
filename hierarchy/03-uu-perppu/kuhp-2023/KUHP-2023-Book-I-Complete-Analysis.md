# KUHP 2023 BOOK I - GENERAL PROVISIONS
## Complete Analysis of Indonesia's Revolutionary Criminal Law Foundation

### EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**KUHP 2023 (UU No. 1/2023)** represents the most significant criminal law reform in Indonesia's history, transitioning from Dutch colonial principles to Indonesian Pancasila-based criminal justice. Book I establishes the foundational framework that governs all criminal law in Indonesia.

**Key Revolutionary Changes:**
- Indigenous Indonesian legal philosophy integration
- Corporate criminal liability framework
- Restorative justice mechanisms
- Gender-sensitive criminal law principles
- Environmental crime foundations
- Enhanced human rights protections

## ARTICLE-BY-ARTICLE ANALYSIS

### **CHAPTER I - SCOPE AND APPLICATION (Articles 1-9)**

#### **Article 1 - Fundamental Principle**
> *"Criminal provisions in this Code apply to all persons committing criminal acts within Indonesian territory"*

**Revolutionary Change:** Establishes territorial jurisdiction based on Indonesian sovereignty, not colonial administrative boundaries.

**Core Analysis:**
- Replaces Dutch *legality principle* with Indonesian constitutional framework
- Integrates Pancasila values into criminal law application
- Establishes Indonesian legal personality as foundation for criminal responsibility

#### **Article 2 - Indonesian Criminal Jurisdiction**
**Territorial Application:**
- All Indonesian territory (land, sea, airspace)
- Indonesian flagged vessels and aircraft
- Indonesian embassies and consulates

**Revolutionary Aspects:**
- Expanded maritime jurisdiction reflecting archipelagic state concept
- Cultural sensitivity in remote areas application
- Traditional law (adat) integration mechanisms

#### **Article 3 - Active Personality Principle**
**Key Provisions:**
- Indonesian citizens abroad for serious crimes
- Public officials committing crimes abroad
- Crimes against Indonesian state security

**Innovation:** Balances Indonesian jurisdiction with international cooperation

#### **Article 4 - Passive Personality Principle**
**Coverage:**
- Crimes against Indonesian citizens abroad
- Crimes against Indonesian state interests
- Terrorism and transnational crimes

#### **Article 5 - Protective Principle**
**State Security Focus:**
- Crimes threatening Indonesian sovereignty
- Economic crimes affecting national stability
- Cyber crimes against Indonesian infrastructure

#### **Article 6 - Universal Jurisdiction**
**International Crimes:**
- Genocide
- Crimes against humanity
- War crimes
- Terrorism
- Trafficking in persons

**Revolutionary Aspect:** Full integration with international criminal law

#### **Article 7 - Temporal Application**
**Non-Retroactivity Principle:**
- Criminal provisions apply from effective date
- Exception: laws favorable to defendant
- Transitional provisions for ongoing cases

#### **Article 8 - Legal Person Jurisdiction**
**Corporate Criminal Liability:**
- Corporations as criminal subjects
- Liability of legal entities
- Board and management responsibility

**Revolutionary Innovation:** First comprehensive corporate criminal liability in Indonesian law

#### **Article 9 - Military Jurisdiction Interface**
**Civilian-Military Coordination:**
- Military court jurisdiction limits
- Civilian oversight mechanisms
- Human rights protections for military justice

### **CHAPTER II - CRIMINAL ACTS AND CRIMINAL LIABILITY (Articles 10-28)**

#### **Article 10 - Definition of Criminal Act**
> *"A criminal act is an act that is prohibited by a criminal provision and is threatened with punishment"*

**Revolutionary Elements:**
- Integration of legal, social, and moral wrongfulness
- Cultural sensitivity in defining criminal acts
- Proportionality principle in criminalization

#### **Article 11 - Forms of Criminal Acts**
**Classification:**
1. **Crimes (Kejahatan)** - Serious offenses
2. **Violations (Pelanggaran)** - Minor offenses
3. **Special Criminal Acts** - Separate legislation crimes

#### **Article 12 - Criminal Liability Prerequisites**
**Elements Required:**
1. **Legal Element (Objektif)**: Prohibited act exists
2. **Mental Element (Subjektif)**: Intent or negligence
3. **Unlawfulness**: No justification exists
4. **Accountability**: Person responsible for actions

**Revolutionary Approach:** Integrates Indonesian legal philosophy with modern criminal law theory

#### **Article 13 - Intent (Kesengajaan)**
**Types of Intent:**
1. **Direct Intent**: Purposeful commission
2. **Indirect Intent**: Knowing consequences likely
3. **Conditional Intent**: Accepting possible consequences

#### **Article 14 - Negligence (Kealpaan)**
**Standard:** Failure to exercise reasonable care expected in circumstances

**Innovation:** Contextual negligence standard considering Indonesian social conditions

#### **Article 15 - Age of Criminal Responsibility**
**Provisions:**
- **Under 12 years**: No criminal responsibility
- **12-18 years**: Juvenile justice system
- **Adult responsibility**: 18 years and above

**Revolutionary Aspect:** Child-friendly juvenile justice integration

#### **Article 16 - Mental Incapacity Defense**
**Criteria:**
- Mental illness affecting rational judgment
- Temporary mental incapacity
- Developmental disabilities

**Innovation:** Comprehensive mental health considerations with treatment options

#### **Article 17 - Corporate Criminal Liability**
**Groundbreaking Provisions:**
- Corporations can be held criminally liable
- Board of directors responsibility
- Due diligence defense availability
- Successor liability in mergers

**Revolutionary Impact:** Establishes Indonesia as leader in corporate criminal accountability

#### **Article 18 - Superior Orders Defense**
**Limited Application:**
- Unlawful orders provide no defense
- Military context considerations
- Duty to disobey illegal orders

#### **Article 19 - Necessity Defense**
**Requirements:**
- Imminent danger to life or property
- No reasonable alternative available
- Proportional response to threat

#### **Article 20 - Self-Defense**
**Legitimate Defense:**
- Protection of self or others
- Proportional force requirement
- Immediacy of threat

#### **Article 21 - Consent Defense**
**Valid Consent:**
- Informed and voluntary
- Within legal limits
- Not contrary to public order

#### **Article 22 - Attempt**
**Punishable Attempts:**
- Clear intent to commit crime
- Direct acts toward completion
- Failure due to external circumstances

**Punishment:** Up to 2/3 of completed crime penalty

#### **Article 23 - Preparation**
**Limited Criminalization:**
- Only for serious crimes specifically designated
- Clear preparatory acts
- Substantial step toward commission

#### **Article 24 - Withdrawal from Crime**
**Voluntary Withdrawal:**
- Genuine abandonment of criminal enterprise
- Before point of no return
- May reduce or eliminate punishment

#### **Article 25 - Participation in Crime**
**Forms of Participation:**
1. **Principal**: Direct perpetrator
2. **Inciter**: Encourages commission
3. **Assistant**: Helps before or during crime
4. **Facilitator**: Provides means or opportunity

#### **Article 26 - Continuing Crimes**
**Single vs. Multiple Offenses:**
- Continuing course of conduct
- Single criminal plan
- Related criminal acts

#### **Article 27 - Compound Crimes**
**Multiple Criminal Acts:**
- Concurrent punishment principles
- Absorption principle application
- Cumulative sentencing limits

#### **Article 28 - Statute of Limitations**
**Prosecution Limits:**
- **Light crimes**: 2 years
- **Moderate crimes**: 6 years  
- **Serious crimes**: 12 years
- **No limitation**: Crimes against humanity

### **CHAPTER III - CRIMINAL SANCTIONS (Articles 29-50)**

#### **Article 29 - Types of Criminal Sanctions**
**Revolutionary Two-Track System:**

**A. Primary Sanctions:**
1. **Death penalty** (very limited, with life sentence alternative)
2. **Life imprisonment**
3. **Imprisonment** (minimum 1 day, maximum 20 years)
4. **Confinement** (maximum 1 year)
5. **Criminal fine** (minimum Rp 25,000)
6. **Community service** (20-240 hours)

**B. Additional Sanctions:**
1. Revocation of specific rights
2. Confiscation of assets
3. Publication of judgment
4. Payment of compensation
5. Fulfillment of customary obligations

**Revolutionary Innovation:** Integration of restorative justice through customary obligations

#### **Article 30 - Death Penalty Limitations**
**Revolutionary Restrictions:**
- 10-year moratorium on executions
- Automatic review after 10 years
- Life sentence alternative available
- Prohibited for minors, pregnant women, mentally ill
